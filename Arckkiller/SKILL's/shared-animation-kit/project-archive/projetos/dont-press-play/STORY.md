# Don't Press Play — directed beats

The Parcelarium is one persistent room with a shared engine and three operating levels. Ink handles the lower route, Cyan works the middle conveyors and Amber approaches the upper control panel. The different levels keep simultaneous actions legible and provide explicit platform boundaries.

## 0:00–0:30 — Just one test

The friends prepare the workshop. Amber physically presses PLAY. The engine engages and pneumatic heads feed wrapped parcels into their hands. The friends begin carrying and redirecting deliveries. The button's shutter snaps shut; the test is now running itself.

## 0:30–1:00 — Wrong way

The factory enters REVERSE mode. Conveyors and signal pulses change direction. Each friend turns, braces and redirects a parcel while the others keep moving. Packages collide with the same platform and character geometry used in the drawing, rather than passing through bodies.

## 1:00–1:30 — Express delivery

The machine switches to EXPRESS. Transfers keep arriving as the friends traverse their workstations, carry loads, release parcels and jump where the overhead clearance allows it. The shared engine and delivery count keep advancing.

## 1:30–2:00 — Return to sender

The RETURN LOOP activates a tall return hose. Incoming and outgoing parcels compete for space; free parcels bounce against silhouettes and supports. Actions continue on all three levels.

## 2:00–2:30 — Too much ribbon

RIBBON JAM adds moving strands between the routes. The friends keep working, but the growing tangle makes the automatic process an increasingly bad idea. They begin moving toward the three safety controls.

## 2:30–3:00 — Three, two, pull

Each friend takes a reachable safety lever. The levers resist with spring-like motion while the machine still runs. The three finally pull together at 2:48. The engine and belts coast to a stop, and the original shutter releases.

## 3:00–3:30 — The last delivery

A much larger gift enters from the return side after shutdown. The friends keep moving as it falls and bounces through the workshop. Celebration and confetti meet the final moving delivery. The closing warning reads: “PLEASE DON’T PRESS PLAY.”

The seven 30-second output parts are production review units. They do not reset the room or the story.
