Rosa said my name.

I almost shouted hers.

The officer covered the radio speaker with his hand. Above us, something scraped along the pavement. A shoe dragged. Stopped. Dragged again.

I held the button close to my mouth.

I'm here.

For a moment, I heard nothing but her breathing.

Then she said, stay below the road. I'll get you across.

The canal separated us from a row of dark buildings. Somewhere among them, Rosa was sitting beside a microphone. I searched every window until the officer pulled me back beneath the concrete embankment.

Finding her voice hadn't made the street any safer.

The maintenance worker could barely put weight on his injured leg. The older woman had taken his bag, but every few steps we still had to stop.

Rosa guided us toward a pumping station beneath the road. Its upper office was empty. The inspection steps outside disappeared into black water.

The lower crossing was flooded.

We couldn't reach her yet.

By the time we secured the office door, the light outside had faded. We spent our second night sitting against the walls, sharing the water we had left.

The worker slept in short bursts. The officer watched the door.

I kept the radio against my chest.

Whenever the static changed, I woke.

Look across the canal. Second floor. The narrow window.

I raised my hand.

Other hand, she said.

I switched.

She laughed once, quietly, and then stopped herself.

I hadn't realized how badly I needed to hear a sound like that.

The third morning arrived without sunlight. Smoke covered the rooftops, turning the canal the color of dirty metal.

In the pale light, we could finally see the main bridge.

A bus stood across its entrance. Beyond it, part of the roadway had collapsed. People moved between the abandoned vehicles, but from that distance I couldn't tell who was searching for a way through and who was simply following the noise.

Rosa told us to stop looking at it.

This time, it was a promise.

Rosa.

No glass between us now.

No static.

I couldn't remember any of them.

There was a small shelter beyond the chamber.

Someone had marked the drinking water in careful pencil lines.

The place smelled of damp clothes and boiled food.

I had spent two days looking for somewhere safe.

Standing there, I realized how little I needed it to look like safety.
